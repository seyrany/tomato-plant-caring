# Sources & Evidence Policy

This repository is designed to be a practical guide, but the care recommendations should remain traceable to credible horticultural sources.

## Evidence rules for this repository

Accepted sources:

1. **Rutgers NJAES / Rutgers Cooperative Extension** — preferred for New Jersey-specific recommendations.
2. **Other land-grant university extension publications** — acceptable when Rutgers does not cover a topic sufficiently.
3. **Published regional horticulture books** written by qualified horticulture educators or experienced specialists.

Not accepted as evidence:

- Reddit or forum posts;
- social media;
- anonymous gardening websites;
- product marketing pages used as horticultural evidence;
- unsourced AI-generated care instructions; or
- personal anecdotes presented as general recommendations.

If future contributors add a recommendation, they should add its source to the page where the recommendation appears.

# Primary New Jersey sources

## Rutgers NJAES FS678 — Growing Tomatoes in the Home Garden

**Authors:** Steve Reiners and Peter Nitzsche  
**Publisher:** Rutgers New Jersey Agricultural Experiment Station / Cooperative Extension  
**Use in this repository:** sunlight, soil, pH, varieties, transplanting, spacing, watering, fertilizer, mulch, staking/caging, pruning tradeoffs, disorders, and harvest-season growth.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs678/

## Rutgers NJAES FS681 — Varieties for New Jersey Home Vegetable Gardeners

**Authors:** Wesley Kline, Peter Nitzsche, Michelle Infante-Casella, William Sciarappa, William Hlubik, Mel Henninger, and Raymond Samulis  
**Use in this repository:** cherry tomato varieties that performed well in New Jersey trials.

Cherry varieties listed by Rutgers include Matt's Wild Cherry, Sun Gold, Sugar Snack, Sun Sugar, and Sweet 100.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs681/

## Rutgers NJAES FS129 — Planning a Vegetable Garden

**Authors:** Peter Nitzsche and Steve Reiners  
**Use in this repository:** site selection, 8-hour sunlight guidance for fruiting vegetables, rotation, spacing, air movement, disease resistance, and New Jersey planting months.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs129/

## Rutgers NJAES FS787 — Starting Vegetable Seeds Indoors

**Use in this repository:** starting tomatoes 6–8 weeks before transplant and New Jersey's regional variation in last-frost timing.

Rutgers notes that warm-season planting after frost can range from about April 20 in extreme southern New Jersey to June 1 in colder northwestern areas.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs787/

## Rutgers NJAES FS547 — Diagnosing and Controlling Fungal Diseases of Tomato in the Home Garden

**Authors:** Andy Wyenandt and Peter Nitzsche  
**Use in this repository:** early blight, Septoria leaf spot, anthracnose, late blight, Buckeye rot, sanitation, crop rotation, mulching, irrigation practices, air movement, and disease prevention.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs547/

## Rutgers NJAES FS1124 — Vegetable Disease Recommendations for Home Gardens

**Use in this repository:** disease-management principles, crop rotation, sanitation, plant spacing, watering, and the importance of correct diagnosis.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS1124/

## Rutgers NJAES FS1123 — Vegetable Insect Control Recommendations for Home Gardens

**Updated:** 2026  
**Use in this repository:** scouting frequency, pest identification, integrated pest-management principles, and current home-garden pest controls.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/fs1123/

## Rutgers NJAES FS1389 — Hornworms in the Home Garden

**Authors:** Brianna Fisher and Ruth Carll  
**Published:** 2026  
**Use in this repository:** tomato/tobacco hornworm identification, New Jersey occurrence, scouting, hand removal, frass, beneficial braconid wasps, and management.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS1389/

## Rutgers NJAES FS011 — Blossom End Rot of Tomatoes, Peppers, and Eggplant

**Authors:** Stephen Reiners, Peter Nitzsche, and Wesley Kline  
**Use in this repository:** distinguishing blossom-end rot from infectious disease, calcium/water uptake, moisture stress, root damage, pH testing, and prevention.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/pubs/download.php?strPubID=FS011

## Rutgers NJAES FS055 — Container Gardening with Vegetables

**Authors:** W. Brad Johnson, Charlene Costaris, and Peter Nitzsche  
**Use in this repository:** container variety selection, growing media, drainage, sunlight, frequent watering, fertilizer, and staking container tomatoes.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS055/

## Rutgers NJAES FS1102 — The Stake and Weave Training System for Tomatoes in the Home Garden

**Use in this repository:** stakes, stake-and-weave support, support for indeterminate plants, and sanitation of reusable stakes.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS1102/

## Rutgers NJAES FS626 — Fertilizing the Home Vegetable Garden

**Authors:** Joseph Heckman and Steve Reiners  
**Use in this repository:** soil-test-based fertilizer planning and vegetable-garden fertility.

https://njaes.rutgers.edu/pubs/publication.php?pid=FS626

# Book references

## Grow Great Vegetables in New Jersey

**Author:** Marie Iannotti  
**Publisher:** Timber Press  
**Publication date:** 2019  
**ISBN:** 9781604698855

This New Jersey-specific vegetable gardening book covers climate zones, average frost dates, the growing season, sun, soil, fertilizer, mulch, water, crop rotation, varieties, and a month-by-month garden plan. Iannotti was a Master Gardener Emeritus and a former Cornell Cooperative Extension horticulture educator.

Publisher page: https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/marie-iannotti/grow-great-vegetables-in-new-jersey/9781604698855/

### How it is used here

It is included as a regional book reference and as support for organizing care seasonally. Specific numeric tomato-care recommendations in this repository are preferentially tied to Rutgers fact sheets so readers can verify them freely online.

## Month-by-Month Gardening in New Jersey

**Author:** Pegi Ballister-Howells  
**Publisher:** Cool Springs Press  
**Publication year:** 2005

Rutgers University Libraries lists this title in its New Jersey horticulture bibliography. It is useful as a state-specific seasonal gardening reference.

Rutgers bibliography: https://exhibits.libraries.rutgers.edu/garden-state-harvest/bibliography

# Maintenance note

Pest and pesticide recommendations can change. When updating pesticide-related content, check the **current** Rutgers FS1123/FS1124 and the pesticide label rather than relying on an old edition or cached recommendation.
