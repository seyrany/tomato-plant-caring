# Cherry Tomato Plant Care — New Jersey

A practical, evidence-based guide for growing and caring for cherry tomato plants in New Jersey home gardens and containers.

## Source policy

This repository intentionally uses **only**:

- Rutgers New Jersey Agricultural Experiment Station (NJAES) / Rutgers Cooperative Extension publications;
- other land-grant/university extension guidance when needed; and
- published gardening books with relevant regional expertise.

No Reddit posts, gardening blogs, social-media posts, AI-generated gardening claims, or anonymous web advice are used as horticultural sources.

## New Jersey cherry varieties

Rutgers lists the following cherry tomatoes among varieties that have performed well in New Jersey trials:

- `Matt's Wild Cherry`
- `Sun Gold`
- `Sugar Snack`
- `Sun Sugar`
- `Sweet 100`

Rutgers also lists grape tomatoes such as `Smarty` and `Tami G`. Variety performance can vary by site, year, disease pressure, and garden conditions.

## The shortest possible care checklist

1. Give fruiting tomatoes **at least 8 hours of direct sun** when possible.
2. Grow in well-drained soil and, ideally, test the soil before fertilizing or liming.
3. Keep moisture **consistent**; Rutgers recommends at least about **1 inch of water per week** for garden-grown tomatoes from rainfall plus irrigation, with slow, deep watering.
4. Water the soil rather than routinely wetting foliage; morning watering and soaker/drip-style irrigation help foliage dry and reduce conditions favorable to disease.
5. Mulch after the soil warms to reduce moisture swings and soil splash.
6. Support plants with a cage, stake, or trellis. Many cherry tomatoes are vigorous indeterminate plants and can grow tall.
7. Do not assume more fertilizer is better. Excess nitrogen can produce lots of foliage and few fruits.
8. Inspect plants at least a couple of times each week for pests and disease symptoms.
9. Pick ripe fruit regularly and remove severely diseased plant debris from the garden.
10. Expect indeterminate tomatoes to keep producing until frost; tomatoes are frost-sensitive.

> **Important:** Pesticide labels and registrations change. If chemical control is ever needed, use the current Rutgers recommendation and follow the product label exactly. The label is the law.

## Primary authority

For a New Jersey home grower, the primary authority in this repository is **Rutgers NJAES / Rutgers Cooperative Extension**, because its recommendations are written for New Jersey conditions.
