# Growing Cherry Tomatoes in Containers

Cherry tomatoes can grow well in containers, but pots dry out and lose nutrients faster than garden soil. Container care therefore needs closer monitoring.

## Choose the right plant

Rutgers recommends compact or dwarf vegetable varieties for containers because very vigorous plants can outgrow restricted spaces. A vigorous indeterminate cherry tomato can still be grown in a pot, but it will need a larger root volume and strong support.

## Light

Fruiting vegetables such as tomatoes need about **8 hours of direct sun** according to Rutgers. A balcony or patio that receives only a few hours of light may keep a plant alive but can limit flowering and fruit production.

## Drainage

The container must drain freely. Waterlogged roots lack oxygen and can contribute to stress and blossom-end-rot problems.

Rutgers recommends a well-aerated container growing medium rather than simply filling pots with dense garden soil.

## Watering

Container tomatoes may need watering much more often than in-ground plants. Rutgers notes that container vegetables can sometimes require watering **two or three times in a day** under demanding conditions.

That is not a command to water on a fixed schedule. Instead:

1. Check the growing medium regularly.
2. Water thoroughly when it is drying rather than giving tiny surface splashes.
3. Make sure excess water can drain.
4. Expect hot, sunny, windy days to increase demand.
5. Do not allow repeated severe wilting followed by flooding.

## Fertilizer

Because nutrients can leach from containers, Rutgers recommends regular water-soluble fertilizer for container vegetables. Follow the current product label and the Rutgers container guidance rather than making a stronger mix.

Avoid excessive nitrogen: a huge leafy tomato plant is not necessarily a productive tomato plant.

## Support

Install a cage, stake, or trellis early. A container is easier to tip over than a garden bed, so make sure both the support and the pot itself are stable.

## Heat around buildings

Rutgers cautions that masonry surfaces can reflect heat. On a balcony or patio, watch for signs that the pot and plant are overheating or drying unusually quickly near hot walls.

## Container troubleshooting

### Plant wilts every afternoon but recovers after watering

The container may be too small for the plant's root mass or may be drying too quickly. Increase monitoring and consider whether a larger container is appropriate.

### Leaves are lush but there are few flowers or fruits

Review nitrogen feeding and light exposure. Rutgers lists excessive nitrogen and temperature extremes among reasons tomatoes can develop foliage without setting fruit.

### Fruit bottoms turn black and leathery

Treat this first as a moisture/root-uptake problem, not automatically as proof that the potting mix lacks calcium. Keep moisture more even and ensure drainage is functioning.

### Fruit cracks

Try to reduce large swings between very dry and very wet growing medium.
